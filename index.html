In a year, councils in Australia host thousands of small to large scale local events, such as reading sessions for children, craft classes and workshops.

They are sessions where communities gather and meet. But which events do people actually take part in, and who are the events for?

This visualisation follows the data in three steps: where people take part, who the events serve and when they run, and the council's strategy on bringing people in.

Who exactly are the events for, and who ends up missing out those events due to the timing?

Two cities help answer this. Brisbane publishes every event it runs, but they do not record how many came to each.

Meanwhile, Townsville counts the people at each library session. So both are used to estimate the timing and participation aspects of events.

Conclusion

Councils get Australians taking part by making it easily accessible, for example, events that are free, close to home and walk-ins.

Most of the events have a focus on children and seniors which largely promotes family participation while giving priority activities to the elderly.
