How do councils get Australians taking part?

Council events across Australia

Councils host small local events, such as reading sessions for children, craft classes and workshops, where neighbours meet and get to know each other.

Australian councils run thousands of them every year. But which events do people actually take part in, and who is left out?

This visualisation follows the data in three steps: where people take part, who events serve and when they run, and how councils bring more people in.

Where are events hosted?

How often do Australians take part, and where? Libraries are one of the few council services that count who comes.

This lets us compare whole states and single councils, from big cities to small outback towns.

Attendance counts each visit, so a parent who comes every week is counted many times.

Who is the target audience?

Councils run plenty of events. The real differences are in who they are for, and who ends up missing out.

Two cities help answer this. Brisbane publishes every event it runs, but not how many people came to each.

Townsville counts the people at each library session. So Brisbane shows what is on offer, and Townsville who turns up.

What works

So what do councils do to make taking part easier for residents, and does any of it actually work?

The first three charts look at cost, distance and taking sessions out to where people already are.

The last chart asks a different question: beyond turning up, do residents feel their council listens?

So, how do councils do it?

Councils get Australians taking part by making it easy: events that are free, close to home and need no booking.

They do best when they take events out of the library to where people already are, like parks and schools.

But the timetable still decides who is invited, and not everyone, especially in the country, feels heard.

Lower the barriers

Nearly eight in ten Brisbane events are free, about half are both free and walk-in, and libraries and parks across the suburbs host seven in ten of them.

Go to the people

Expos and pop-up sessions held outside the library draw the biggest crowds. In Townsville, park sessions were 2% of library sessions but brought in 17% of the people.

Mind the gaps

Most events run on weekday mornings, so school-age children, teenagers and working adults miss out, and country Victorians feel less heard than four years ago.

About the data

State totals are for 2023–24, council figures for 2024–25 and events for 2025.

Council figures cover New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland. Other states do not publish them council by council.

Brisbane's events have no attendance. Townsville's attendance covers library programs only.

Brisbane's full year of events was read from the council's calendar feed on 10 October 2026.
